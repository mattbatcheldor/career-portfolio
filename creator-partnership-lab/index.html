The buying decision

Dinner is the problem.
Creators are the hypothesis.

Weeknight Table is an imagined recipe-kit business. The question is whether creator-led demonstrations could make its product useful and credible in a busy person's routine.

£18Assumed kit for two
25-44Proposed adult audience
6 weeksOverall programme
£50kIllustrative envelope

The business hypothesis

Less planning could be a compelling reason to try a meal kit. The proposed audience, price and need are starting assumptions to validate through discovery.

The decision to fund

A contained test with defined outputs, rights and commercial terms. Creator quotes, audience data and product testing would precede any commitment.

The client conversation

Who buys now? What has failed? Who owns the decision? What does a customer contribute after fulfilment, discounts and repeat purchase?

The success condition

Agree the commercial objective and affordable acquisition cost before launch. Popular videos alone would not justify a larger programme.

The food advertising constraint

The UK restrictions on paid online advertising of identifiable less healthy food or drink products took effect on 5 January 2026. Applicability depends on the product category, nutrient profiling, advertiser status, advertising content and applicable exemptions.

Weeknight Table is fictional. Before briefing or booking, qualified client reviewers would determine which products and proposed ads are eligible. The assessment must consider the specified product categories and nutrient profiling, alongside any advertiser exemptions. Brand advertising has a defined exemption with conditions; it is not an automatic fallback for an ineligible product campaign.

If the proposed work is restricted, revise the product selection or creative route only after review, or stop. Other advertising rules, including disclosure and claims, still apply.

Government guidance on less healthy food advertising ↗

My call: Agree customer economics, tracking readiness and product eligibility before committing the campaign budget. Acquisition is the proposed objective, subject to discovery.

How I would move from shortlist to selection

Audit 10 recent comparable posts for each preferred candidate on the intended platform. Record URLs, publication dates, format, duration, visible views, interactions, disclosure and integration. Separate sponsored and organic work. Use the median rather than a viral outlier; hidden metrics remain unknown.

Proposed selection weights: audience relevance 30%, creative fit 25%, comparable performance 20%, commercial conflicts 15%, delivery practicality 10%. Do not assign a final score without the evidence. Request first-party audience data, current rates, conflicts and availability before booking.

My call: Audit Poppy Cooks, Bored of Lunch and Sorted Food first. Assess audience fit, recent content and commercial conflicts before choosing. Keep Mob as a publisher alternative.

The proposal

Three ways to
rescue a Tuesday.

Each creator brings a different midweek situation. The brand supplies the product and the facts; the creator helps decide how the story should be told.

  1. Three original vertical videosOne per creator, published on the agreed creator channel. YouTube Shorts is the intended lead platform, subject to the content and audience audit.
  2. Six paid-media adaptationsTwo versions per original. Editing, music and talent permissions must cover the planned use. Cross-platform posts are separately scoped.
  3. A capped distribution testPaid activity in weeks 4 and 5 of the overall six-week programme, within a £15,000 starting allowance, featuring only products and advertising executions confirmed eligible after client review. Adequate sample size and tracking must be confirmed.
  4. A decision at the endReview delivery, audience response, customer signals and costs. Decide whether to extend one creator, change the proposition or stop.
Read the draft creator brief

Audience problem: dinner feels like another task after a busy day. Desired response: investigate whether the kit fits their routine.

Required: demonstrate the actual product, preparation and meal; use only verified claims; disclose the commercial relationship clearly. Product testing must support any time-saving claim.

Creative freedom: the situation, recipe, humour and storytelling style. “It is Tuesday, I am hungry, and I have no dinner plan” is a conversation starter, not an imposed script.

Approval scope: one consolidated concept review and one factual/compliance edit review. Essential factual or compliance corrections must be resolved before publication. Other additional changes trigger a scope decision.

My call: Be precise about facts, claims, disclosure and deliverables, while giving creators room to shape the execution. Include two consolidated review rounds, with a process for essential corrections.

Starting model: £50,000 total. Agency fee is £8,000.

What needs agreeing before booking?

Specify outputs, territory, platform, paid-use duration, editing rights, exclusivity, music, approvals, cancellation and expiry. The starting proposal would seek a quote for six weeks of UK paid use. Perpetual or EMEA rights are separately priced options.

If creator quotes exceed the allowance, revise count, outputs, rights or distribution before discounting. If the client wants 20% off, build a genuinely smaller £40,000 scope and check delivery costs again.

The client envelope is not the agency fee. The model isolates fee contribution; it does not claim whole-campaign gross margin or define an accounting revenue policy. Unspent reserve is reconciled, not assumed earned.

My call: Protect the rights and measurement needed for the agreed test. Reduce scope if quotes exceed the allowance. Buy the permissions required for the test, rather than automatically buying broad rights.

The evidence standard

What would persuade
you to renew?

Organic views, paid distribution and customer behaviour answer different questions. Keep them separate, agree attribution windows and avoid presenting a tracked sale as proof of incremental sales.

QuestionEvidenceDecision
Did people watch?Platform-defined views, watch time and completion for comparable formats.Keep or revise the creative opening.
Was the audience relevant?First-party geography and age where available, plus relevant comment themes.Adjust selection or audience strategy.
Did people investigate?Tagged qualified visits, engaged sessions and recipe-page actions.Improve the proposition or destination.
Did customers buy?Attributed first orders, acquisition cost, refunds and an agreed window.Compare with finance's affordability limit.
Did creators add value?A controlled paid experiment, if the setup and sample permit it.Decide whether another test is justified.
How would I design the paid experiment?

Google's guidance recommends comparing a regular video campaign with a campaign adding creator video, keeping other settings consistent. Confirm measurement access, adequate sample and rights before promising a statistically reliable result. Technical video access does not replace permission to use content in advertising.

Read Google's creator partnership guidance ↗

My call: Judge acquisition against an agreed affordability limit and evidence quality. Assess repeat purchase over a suitable follow-up period. Two weeks of paid activity may not establish a reliable winner.

Winning the business

How the programme
gets bought.

A pilot is approved by several people at once. Each needs a different reason to say yes, and any one of them can stop it late.

Marketing lead

Owns the objective and the budget. Needs a credible route to growth and a clear story for their own leadership.

Growth or performance team

Judges creators against existing paid channels. Needs cost per first order compared with what they already buy.

Finance and procurement

Needs a capped scope, transparent pass-through costs, a clear exit point and no open-ended rights spend.

Legal and compliance

Needs disclosure, claim substantiation, less healthy food classification and usage terms settled before anything is filmed.

Six discovery questions

  1. What does a new customer need to be worth after fulfilment, discounts and repeat purchase?
  2. What has the brand tried with creators or paid social, and why did it stop?
  3. Who signs off spend, and who could stop a campaign after approval?
  4. Which kits have been checked against the less healthy food rules?
  5. What first-party data and tracking can the brand share during the pilot?
  6. What would make a second phase an easy decision for you?
Three likely objections, and how I would answer them

“Creators are expensive for what you get.” That is why the proposed pilot is capped. Discovery determines how many routes the budget and sample can support. Further investment depends on evidence against your acquisition limit.

“Why not run this through our own paid social?” If feasible, a controlled paid experiment would compare creator video with your standard video while holding other settings consistent. We would confirm the sample and measurement setup before promising a reliable answer.

“Can you do it for £40,000?” I would re-scope against confirmed quotes, likely starting with two creators. The revised proposal would retain the rights and measurement required for its objective, with delivery costs checked before commitment.

From pilot to partnership

  1. PilotUp to six weeks, subject to discovery. Creator count and test design depend on the audit, quotes and sample. Illustrative £50,000 envelope.
  2. Second phaseIf evidence supports it, extend a promising route as a recurring series for a quarter, with rights planned for the agreed period.
  3. Always onA retained creator roster and annual rights planning only if sustained results justify them. Assess one further market before wider expansion.

My call: Make the pilot straightforward to approve through clear scope, transparent costs and decision gates. Recommend renewal only when the evidence supports it.

Commercial delivery

A promise the team
can deliver.

Before week 1

Agree the commitment

Agree the discovery scope, fee, decision criteria and approval owners. Campaign booking remains conditional on the discovery findings.

Week 1

Validate the people and product

Finish the audit, review first-party data, test the product and assess eligibility and measurement feasibility. Proceed, revise or stop; confirm campaign scope and contracts only if viable.

Week 2

Develop the concepts

Creators shape the idea. Client feedback is consolidated and production checks feasibility.

Week 3

Produce and approve

Check facts, disclosure, music, usage permissions and measurement readiness before publication.

Weeks 4 and 5

Publish and learn

Run the agreed distribution test. Record live URLs, spend, performance, changes and emerging issues.

Week 6

Review the account

Reconcile delivery and cost, explain evidence limits, and recommend renewal, revision or stopping.

How does this become an account growth plan?

Move from a qualified discovery to a proposal, negotiated scope and signed agreement with a next action, owner and decision date. Forecast the agency fee separately from pass-through costs; weighted pipeline is not cash or recognised revenue.

At review, identify a specific next problem. A renewal could extend one successful creator or test a new creative route. An EMEA extension should begin with one additional market, local research and appropriate delivery support.

What checks matter for a real campaign?

Use the current ASA guidance on clear advertising disclosure. Before any paid food activity, confirm each featured product's status under the less healthy food advertising rules, plus applicable audience, placement and claim restrictions, with qualified client reviewers. Check licences and approvals for each intended use.
